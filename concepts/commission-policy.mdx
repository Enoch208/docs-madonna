title: "Commission policies"
description: "Learn how reward rates, caps, weights, short paths, and versioned terms determine a sale’s allocation."

Every campaign has a published commission policy. The policy defines the amount the merchant is willing to reward and how that amount is divided across eligible contributors.

## The policy fields

| Field                  | Meaning                                              |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| `commissionBps`        | Reward rate in basis points; 1,000 means 10%         |
| `perSaleCap`           | Maximum reward for an individual sale                |
| `campaignLiabilityCap` | Campaign-wide bound on reward liability              |
| `maxRewardedAncestors` | Maximum paid levels, from 1 to 5                     |
| `weightsBps`           | Nearest-first split, totaling 10,000 basis points    |
| `shortPathMode`        | How to handle fewer eligible people than paid levels |
| `holdSeconds`          | Refund hold before a sale can finalize               |
| Policy hash            | Commitment to the exact version accepted by a sale   |

Money is calculated in integer token base units. The API represents it as decimal strings, avoiding floating-point rounding.

## A worked example

This is an illustrative policy, not an offer or revenue claim:

- Product price: **100 SDUSD**.
- Reward rate: **10%**, with a per-sale cap high enough to allow **10 SDUSD**.
- Four paid levels with nearest-first weights **50% / 25% / 15% / 10%**.
- Sufficient funded budget and remaining campaign liability capacity.

| Recipient on the purchase path | Weight | Reward     |
| ------------------------------ | ------ | ---------- |
| Nearest contributor            | 50%    | 5.00 SDUSD |
| One level above                | 25%    | 2.50 SDUSD |
| Two levels above               | 15%    | 1.50 SDUSD |
| Three levels above             | 10%    | 1.00 SDUSD |

The **100 SDUSD buyer payment** and the **10 SDUSD merchant-funded reward** are separate. The reward is not silently deducted from the buyer payment.

## Short paths

If a sale has fewer eligible contributors than the policy's maximum, the configured short-path rule determines the result:

- **Renormalize existing:** divide the reward proportionally across the eligible weights. With only the first two people in the example, the ratio is 50:25, or approximately two-thirds and one-third.
- **Return unused:** allocate the existing levels' original shares and leave the unused share in the merchant's reward budget.

Integer rounding is deterministic. Any allocation remainder is assigned to the nearest eligible contributor. The application preview shows the actual amounts for the current path.

## Budget and liability are real constraints

A percentage alone does not promise a payout. The contract also checks the funded budget, policy caps, campaign state, and path eligibility. A campaign that cannot meet its required reward reservation cannot accept the purchase under those terms.

Direct purchases with `nodeId = 0` reserve no distributor reward.

## Changing a policy

A merchant can register **one upcoming version at a time**, with **at least 24 hours' notice**. The version becomes eligible at its effective time. Existing purchases retain the policy and allocations they accepted; later policy changes do not rewrite them.

The hold period, rate, and path on the receipt are the terms to inspect when reviewing an existing sale.
